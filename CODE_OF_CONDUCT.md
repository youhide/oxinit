# Code of Conduct

This applies to issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, and any
other space belonging to this project.

## Expected

- Argue about the code, not the person who wrote it.
- Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith. Most
  disagreements in a project like this are missing context, not bad intent.
- Be specific. "This races with the reaper when a service forks during stop" is
  useful. "This is wrong" is not.
- Accept that maintainers may reject a change that works. Scope and
  maintenance cost are legitimate reasons.
- Accept a technical correction without escalating. Being wrong about a syscall
  is not an insult.
- Ask when you do not understand something. Nobody here was born knowing how
  cgroup v2 works.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, in public or private. This includes sustained unwanted contact
  after being asked to stop.
- Insults or personal attacks, including ones framed as technical criticism.
- Demeaning comments about someone's identity, background, or experience level.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission.
- Deliberately derailing threads, or reopening settled decisions to exhaust
  people rather than to persuade them.

Being technically correct does not exempt you from any of this. Neither does
being a long-time contributor.

## Reporting

Email **youri@youhide.com.br**. Include what happened, where, and links if the
exchange was public.

Reports are read by the maintainer only. Your identity is not shared with the
person you are reporting without your agreement. If the report concerns the
maintainer, there is currently no separate escalation path — the project is
small enough that this is stated honestly rather than papered over.

You will get an acknowledgement. You may not get a detailed account of what
action was taken, since that often involves someone else's private
correspondence.

## Consequences

Depending on severity and history:

1. A private note explaining what was wrong.
2. A public correction in the thread.
3. A temporary block from the project's spaces.
4. A permanent ban.

Maintainers decide, and they are not obliged to start at step 1. A first
offence can be a ban if it is serious enough. Continuing after being asked to
stop escalates on its own.
